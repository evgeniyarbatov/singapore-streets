# Lorong

Malay for "alley" or "lane".[^1] Like [[jalan|Jalan]] it comes first in the name, so Lorong Halus is the Malay form of what English names put as "… [[lane|Lane]]". 208 of the 4,787 streets contain it and 202 start with it. Half are Malay & Archipelago (104) and nearly all the rest Numeric & Functional (101), mostly its numbered series.

## History

The 1967 advisory committee on street naming recommended Malay names to foster a sense of national identity, which explains the city's jalans and lorongs.[^2] For a time roads in new residential areas were given Malay names, often with Jalan or Lorong, until the policy was amended in 1968.[^3] Current guidelines say non-English names such as Lorong and Jalan are to be avoided for new street names.[^4]

## Numbered lorongs

The longest series in the dataset is Lorong N Geylang: a series of lanes branching off both sides of Geylang Road.[^5] Toa Payoh, Changi and Realty Park have their own numbered lorongs, and Telok Kurau has lettered ones such as Lorong G Telok Kurau.

## Themed names

Six streets near Beauty World are named after Malay banana varieties: Lorong Pisang Asam, Batu, Emas, Hijau, Raja and Udang.[^6]

- Why Toa Payoh, a 1960s HDB town, numbered its streets as Lorongs rather than English Streets; one account ties it to Singapore being part of Malaysia when the town was planned, but no NLB, Archives, Roots or URA source was found.
- How Geylang's lorongs were numbered (odd and even sides) and when.
